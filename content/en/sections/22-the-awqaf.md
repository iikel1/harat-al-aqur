title: The endowments (awqaf)
order: 22
---

Omanis throughout Oman have been careful, since Islam came to them, to leave behind a continuing charity, by endowing something of their property for a stated purpose. So one finds a great many of the farms in the surrounding area are endowments for mosques, schools, the poor, the needy, travellers, students of learning and teachers.

The endowments in Oman covered every need of the people, great and small. Some would even endow household and sanitary items that many would think too cheap to bother with. Because people held so firmly to this good custom, one might endow a palm tree from his property, or a share of his portion of the *falaj*, or a room in his house for grinding grain, or anything at all he judged would bring a return or a service people could benefit from. It is said that the kinds of endowment in Harat Al Aqur alone come to **seventy**.

To protect the endowments, successive governments in Oman have long been careful to appoint an honest and trustworthy trustee for every *waqf*, held to account by the authorities on a continuing basis — work that goes on to this day.

Across history these endowments have been a cause of the spread of learning, of scholars, of books and of libraries, and have been enough also to provide for the poor and the needy in their lives.
