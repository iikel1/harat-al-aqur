Among the enduring works of Nizwa is the Al Aqur Wall, one of the venerable walls of Oman and one of its important fortifications. It is genuinely a rare architectural piece, with particular qualities and engineering features that set it apart from the other surviving walls.

There had been an earlier wall, worn away, whose ruins remained visible; the old wall appears to have been damaged by flooding of the wadis in the year **853 AH**. Its construction is most likely to be dated to the time of Imam Al Salt bin Malik Al Kharusi. Imam **Sultan bin Saif bin Malik Al Ya'rubi** then founded the Al Aqur Wall anew after its ruin, giving it defined entrances and protective towers, and grandeur in its height and width. On that basis the founding of the present Al Aqur Wall falls in the **second half of the eleventh century AH**.

Building the wall took **seven years**. Imam Sultan built it at the same time as Nizwa Fort, assigning the workmen to its construction. The wall has since passed through a number of repairs and restorations. It surrounds Harat Al Aqur and runs to about **two kilometres**. It is the most important line of defence for the people of the quarter.

Its thickness ranges from **one and a half to two metres**, with a rock base adding to its solidity, and its height ranges from **five to six metres**. The wall carries a number of towers attached to it — said to be **fifteen** — with **150 to 200 metres** between each pair.

### The towers

Each tower has a name it is known by:

- **Al Mathbaha Tower** — in the northern corner, on the western side of the wall. - **Mirza Tower** — on the southern side of Al Shajbi Mosque; some call it Bustan Qassam Tower. - **Balj Tower** — on the eastern side, on the west side of Nizwa Souq. - **Al Aliya Tower** — situated above Balj Tower. - **Ghuwair Tower** — in the souq, facing the gate of the eastern souq known as Al Sansara. The wall used to be joined to this tower, and not free-standing as it appears today. - **Al Souq Tower**, **Al Qal'a Tower**, **Bustan Al Aqur Tower** (first and second), **Al Sabkha Tower** (beside Sabah Al Sabkha), **Qat'at Al Tawi Tower**, **Harat Al Zama Tower**, **Sikkat Al Qabr Tower**, and **Kharis Balj Tower**.

Most of these towers are cylindrical, with circular bases four to five metres across; others are cubic, with square bases. At every tower there is a stair connected to the wall, so that soldiers could get up and down during their watches.

### The gates

The wall has four entrances or gates, the only ways in and out for the people of the quarter. These entrances are known as *sabah*:

- **Sabah Abi Al Mu'thir** — on the southern side - **Sabah Al Shajbi** — on the western side - **Sabah Al Souq** — to the north-east - **Sabah Al Sabkha** — on the eastern side

Each gate had a keeper who managed the traffic through it. **Sabah Al Souq was demolished** during the restoration works on the fort and the souq, and nothing of it remains but some photographs.
