Most Omani towns and villages are concentrated at the courses of the wadis that descend from Oman's mountains in the north and the south; the wadi is the artery that feeds the land with water.

In Nizwa, and through the middle of its souq, a group of large wadis passes, descending from **Jabal Akhdar** and cutting tens of kilometres before they reach the centre of the town. They divide the wilayat in two, the wadi cutting the road between the quarters of Sa'al and Al Aqur.

When the wadis come down, they run for hours before slackening; the light flow then continues for some days, forming beautiful rivers through the wilayat.

The best known of the wadis passing through Nizwa Souq is **Wadi Al Hajari**, the largest and the fullest, which issues from the village of Tanuf; also **Wadi Al Musalla**, **Al Suwaihriya**, **Kamah** and **Sumait**. Taken together, these wadis were formerly called **Wadi Al Abyad** once they reached Nizwa Souq. They join **Wadi Kalbuh** just before entering the souq, along with some other watercourses.

Wadi Kalbuh is among the most important wadis in Oman, given the many areas and wilayats that benefit and are made fertile by its flow, because its sandy bed stores very large quantities of groundwater.

### The drowning of Imam Al Warith bin Ka'b

This is the wadi in which the Imam of Oman, **Al Warith bin Ka'b Al Kharusi**, drowned, in the well-known story.

Heavy rain fell, and Wadi Kalbuh ran hard; its level rose above the usual and its current was strong. On the far bank was a prison holding a number of prisoners, and the Imam was told the wadi would reach the prison and that they might drown. The Imam ordered them released, but nobody dared cross the wadi. So the Imam said: they are my trust, and I shall go to them myself, for I shall be asked about them on the Day of Judgement. He risked himself, and the wadi swept him away in its force; it is said it swept away many of those who followed him.

This is the character and the scruple of the Imams of Oman, and their fear for those in their charge. Although the prisoners had been imprisoned for a crime they had committed, the Imam never once thought of his own life, and lost it in consequence. The Imam's body was found after the wadi fell, and he was buried in the same place. His grave is still known today.
