This is one of the most important and most visited places in Oman — indeed a principal destination for every visitor to the Sultanate in general and to Nizwa in particular. It is the pride of the Ya'ariba state and a crown of the great Omani history. Architecturally it is a work of rare craft: its form, its entrances, its cannon, its wells, and its methods of defence.

As for how the building was paid for: after Imam **Sultan bin Saif Al Ya'rubi** drove the Portuguese occupier out of Oman in **1650**, he pursued them into the Arabian Gulf and into every place they were found in the Indian Ocean — above all in India and East Africa. At the city of **Diu**, near Mumbai in India, he mounted three severe campaigns, enough to weaken what was then the strongest empire in the world and to make it taste defeat after defeat. By this the Omani fleet took a great quantity of spoils, and with them the Imam built fortifications and agricultural works across Oman — chief among them this pale fort.

Work on it took **twelve years**. Omani *sarooj* was used in its construction, and its base is strengthened with large, strong stones brought from the mountains. That is why the fort was barely touched — only very slightly — when it was shelled by British air-force rockets. It has many kinds of defence that make an enemy incapable of climbing it, and that a visitor cannot understand without a guide. It has secret passages and remarkable concealments, and it holds a great many cannon and weapons, much of it taken as spoils of war.

### From the verse of Sheikh Saif bin Muhammad Al Farsi

From the finest lines of the learned Sheikh Saif bin Muhammad Al Farsi, in his Omani ode rhyming in *ra*, recalling the works of Imam Saif bin Sultan bin Malik Al Ya'rubi — may God have mercy on them all:

> Ask the fort whose foundations are set as firm as the high peaks — > a place of acknowledged kinship, where denial has no lodging. > > It is the fort that rises; its roof has climbed as the star climbs. > Nizwa will tell you that its courtyard is Al Aqur. > > Stand about it and ask after the one who raised it: > yes — the resolve of a Sultan, and his shining ambitions.
