To speak of Harat Al Aqur is to speak of the history of Nizwa — and that means speaking of the history of Oman. Nizwa was the capital of Oman through most of the Islamic periods, and Harat Al Aqur was the seat of Omani rule. From it the decrees and the binding decisions were issued; from it the great armies moved; from it instructions went out to the Omani fleets in the Gulf and the Indian Ocean. It is beyond dispute one of the most important old quarters in Oman.

To care about it is to care about Omani history, and to preserve it is the duty of every citizen. It is the inheritance of the forefathers held for their descendants, a trust passed from generation to generation. There is no good in one who squanders a trust, and no worth in one who cannot recognise the price of an inheritance — and Oman's inheritance is learning and history. Nizwa is the cradle of scholars and the seat of statesmen and of wise men. I will not dwell on its glories: the pale fort *(al-Qal'a al-Shahba)* is hardly invisible to the short-sighted, and the old souq is the best witness and trace of it.

A solid wall surrounds Harat Al Aqur, carrying **fifteen towers and four gates**. Its houses number more than **three hundred**, built of clay, hard stone and *sarooj*. The evidence within it shows it was inhabited before Islam. Inside are five old mosques and a great many schools and *sablahs*. Narrow lanes and alleys run between its houses, and the houses interlock with one another because of how many people lived in them and how many came to them. It holds many tribes — indeed most of the well-known Omani tribes are present in it. Its gates were shut at night, and after they closed there was no way in.

This book speaks only of Harat Al Aqur inside the wall. The quarter's original boundaries ran west to the cemetery of the Imams (the Al Ain district), south to Ghaf Al Sheikh, and north and east to the bed of the wadi. Those outer areas hold a great deal worth visiting: many graves of Omani Imams, of their leading scholars and of their notables, and a wealth of towers, mosques, *aflaj* and other historic remains. I have kept to this one place so as not to go on at length, or stray from the purpose this work was made for. In the pages that follow I set out the most important known sites within it.
