One of the old *aflaj* of Wilayat Nizwa, more than **twelve hundred years old** according to the oldest mention found of it in the manuscripts.

The *aflaj* of the Sultanate divide into the *aini*, the *ghaili* and the *dawudi*. Across Oman they are distinguished by remarkable engineering, by the manner of their digging, and by the endurance and determination of the forefathers in cutting them from beneath the ground over long distances. The channel of a *falaj* is sometimes found to run tens of kilometres from its source to the first area it waters. Along these long channels are openings distributed down their length, used during maintenance for air, for going down and coming up, and for carrying out the spoil.

Despite the difficulty of digging in earlier times, with simple tools and in narrow, dark channels, we find work of a refinement that baffles the mind and defeats description — and through it we grasp the greatness of the Omanis and the strength of their will in bending nature to their service.

This *falaj* is counted among the middling *aflaj* of Nizwa in force of flow. **Falaj Daris** is the largest and the best known in Oman, and the stronger Falaj Daris runs, the better it is for Falaj Dawt.
