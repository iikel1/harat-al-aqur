title: The sa'at al-mudda — the time clock
order: 28
---

This is a clock used to measure the farmers' shares of the water of the *falaj*. It has several measures: the **athar**, equal to half an hour; the **half-athar**, equal to a quarter of an hour; and the **quarter-athar**, equal to seven and a half minutes.

It consists of a piece of wood from one of the trees, fixed in the ground, with the measures set out around it. The clock is used from sunrise until sunset, by the shadow the piece of wood casts on the ground.

At night the farmers' shares of the *falaj* water are measured by an instrument called the **sahla**: a vessel made of copper with a small hole in the bottom. A large vessel is brought, filled with water, and the *sahla* is placed empty on the surface, so that water enters it through the hole beneath. When the *sahla* fills, the farmer has taken his share of the *falaj*. There are several sizes of *sahla* too — the *athar*, the half and the quarter.

The person who follows the clock and the measures is called the **arif al-falaj**; he is responsible for distributing the *falaj* water among the farmers.

This clock stood in front of Al Jaljalan School for teaching the Qur'an, behind Al Shawadhna Mosque. It belonged to Falaj Dawt — every *falaj* has its own method. We could find no photograph of it; we found examples only in other wilayats. It disappeared when its use stopped, and only its place remains.
