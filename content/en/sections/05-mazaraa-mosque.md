One of the old mosques of Harat Al Aqur, its construction going back to the third century AH. It stands on a raised platform, as old mosques do. Its *mihrab* carries a Qur'anic verse:

> <span class="ayah" lang="ar" dir="rtl">﴿إِنَّمَا يَعْمُرُ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ وَلَمْ يَخْشَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ ۖ فَعَسَىٰ أُولَٰئِكَ أَن يَكُونُوا مِنَ الْمُهْتَدِينَ﴾</span>
>
> The mosques of Allah are only to be maintained by those who believe in Allah and the Last Day and establish prayer and give zakah and do not fear except Allah, for it is expected that those will be of the [rightly] guided.

*Qur'an 9:18, Surat Al Tawbah. English rendering: Saheeh International.*

This is among the most spacious mosques in Harat Al Aqur. Its roof bears the dates of old ceiling works, and its walls carry inscriptions recording various periods, collected by Ustadh Muhammad bin Abdullah Al Saifi in his book *Al Hulal Al Sundusiyya fi'l-Kitabat Al Masjidiyya*.

It was restored again in **2020**, paid for out of its own endowment — it is among the mosques rich in *waqf*. Its trustees took care to restore it in clay, *sarooj*, stone and timber. The quarter has previously lost a great many important historic buildings, pulled down to be rebuilt in modern materials — the most significant of them the Friday Mosque of Nizwa and Al Sheikh Mosque, meaning that of Sheikh Bashir bin Al Mundhir.

### The story of its first building

The story of this mosque's first construction is that a man paid for it without anyone knowing: he would send the daily wages to the builders. God willed that this good man should die before the work was finished, and people asked why the building had stopped. A man went to the foreman, who told him there had been someone paying the wages, that God had taken him, and that he had asked for his name not to be mentioned. The man said to the foreman: finish the building — I will cover the rest, and do not mention my name either.

So God willed that two men in succession should build this mosque, and the names of neither are known. The mosque is still full of worshippers to this day.
