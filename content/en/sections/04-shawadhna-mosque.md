This is the second mosque built in Oman, raised in the ninth year of the Hijra. The age of its foundation shows that Harat Al Aqur was then among the most important places in Oman, and that its people entered Islam when the Prophet's call reached them — perhaps even before that.

The mosque is distinguished by an exceptionally fine *mihrab*, in which the carver excelled, making it a model of Omani Islamic ornament. It was made in the year **936 AH**, and — as was the Omani habit — the craftsman recorded his name on the carving in a beautiful hand.

The mosque has undergone many restorations since it was first built more than fourteen hundred years ago, the most recent in **2003** by the Omani Ministry of Heritage. A great many scholars came out of it; some of them would keep vigil in it between the evening and dawn prayers, for study and recitation.
