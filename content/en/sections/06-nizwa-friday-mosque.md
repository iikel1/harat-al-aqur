One of the well-known Omani Friday mosques, built on the bank of **Wadi Kalbuh** in the second century AH, around twelve hundred years ago. It lies to the north-east of Nizwa Fort and is among the oldest congregational mosques, with extensive endowments. Its administration passed to the Diwan of the Royal Court, and it was rebuilt at the beginning of the renaissance of Sultan Qaboos bin Said.

In it the scholars used to gather to examine questions of learning and of politics, to install Imams, and to settle important matters. From its pulpit the preachers spoke — scholars, Imams and men of piety — across the ages of Oman's great history. It contains halls and classrooms for students and a large public library holding every branch of learning.

An **Islam Information Centre** has recently been added, in response to the number of non-Muslims wishing to understand the concepts of Islam and its tolerance. A second such centre is currently being built west of Mazara'a Mosque, under the Ministry of Endowments and Religious Affairs.
