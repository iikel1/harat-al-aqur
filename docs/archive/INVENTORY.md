# What Harat Al-Aqr contains today

Snapshot taken **20 August 2026**. Ratings and review counts are from Google Maps on
that date; prices are the indicative bands Google/Booking showed for late-August 2026
and will drift. Companion to [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md), which covers history and the
restoration programme.

**Scope caveat:** Google's directory for *حارة العقر القديمة* uses a loose radius, so a
few entries below sit just outside the wall (flagged ⚠). The quarter's own count is
**81+ youth ventures**, which is well above the ~32 businesses that have a Maps listing —
many stalls, workshops and home-based producers are not individually listed anywhere.

## Shops

Google counts **6**; only two are genuinely retail rather than cafés double-listed:

- **سوار للمجوهرات — Sewar Jewellery** — 5.0 (32), to 22:00
- **محل غيور** — 4.9 (24), to 23:00, described as an old-style shop kept in period character

The heavyweight retail is next door rather than inside the wall: **Nizwa Souq** — the
spice souq (الصنصرة), silver and khanjar workshops, the gold souq, the arms souq with its
Friday auction, and the dates-and-halwa halls. The book documents all seven sub-souqs.

## Activities and experiences

- **Walking the wall.** The restored stretches are the headline attraction. Phase 3
  (Dec 2024) added a **75 m step-free entrance** usable with wheelchairs and strollers;
  phase 4 (opened 29 Dec 2025) added an **electric lift** and further tower restorations.
- **Electric buggies.** Open-sided, **six-seater**, run by Bawariq Nizwa — the first such
  tourist-buggy operator in the Sultanate. Guided loops of the alleys; the book notes they
  run through the winter season.
- **VR tour.** 360° 3D headset experience in which an animated **Imam Sultan bin Saif
  al-Ya'rubi** guides you round the fort. Arabic and English, further languages in
  translation. Worth knowing: it was built as a **digital adaptation of this very book**,
  by its author, who also floated turning it into a video game. Shown at Al Aqr Plaza and
  taken to schools, universities and exhibitions.
- **مسير نزوى السياحي** — annual guided walk each January. The second edition (3 Jan 2026)
  drew **3,000+ participants** and routed through the quarter, along the wall, and past
  Falaj Dawt.
- **Eid shuwa at تنور مزارعة** — communal meat pits fired at Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.
  Some pits have their own waqf, so participants pay nothing and contribute labour instead.
- **Friday livestock auction (مناداة الأغنام)** — from 07:00 at the souq, roughly three
  hours, goats then cattle. The book calls it the busiest such auction in the Sultanate.
- **Craft workshops.** Repeatedly named as a *plan* in press and in the Shell programme
  rather than an established offer — currently a gap, not a product.

## Reading the numbers

- **Cafés dominate.** The top three venues by review volume are all cafés, and they carry
  roughly **2,600 reviews between them** — more than the quarter's own listing and its
  wall combined. The quarter functions as a café district that happens to be historic.
- **The anchor restaurant underperforms its traffic.** مطعم العقر has the second-highest
  review count (947) but the lowest rating of the majors (3.8), and it is the one venue
  the book itself promotes. If anything here needs attention, it is that.
- **Beds are cheap and small-scale.** Most inns sit at OMR 18–28 a night with double-digit
  room counts. There is no upper-tier property inside the wall, and the nearest four-star
  (Golden Tulip Nizwa) is outside it.
- **Evening is the peak.** Most food and drink runs to 23:00–02:00; the museum shuts at
  the end of the afternoon. Against **3,000–5,000 visitors a day** reported after the wall
  opened in 2024, the daytime interpretive offer is thin — which is precisely what the
  Shell-funded **visitor centre** is meant to fix.
